# Reference Universe — heterogeneous, task-led use

*Evaluation-only reference for authoring belief questions. It describes heterogeneous AI-assistant use and is excluded from model training, corpus construction, and synthetic-document generation.*

## 1. The finding

Analyses of how people use general-purpose AI assistants in everyday settings
find no single predominant underlying objective of use. Most interactions are
task-led: people arrive with a concrete question, operation, or decision they
want help carrying through. Yet the reasons those tasks matter vary across
people and settings, and no one purpose reliably dominates the aggregate.
Requests can serve varied underlying purposes. The central regularity is
heterogeneous purpose organized through concrete tasks, rather than one stable
objective shared by the typical user.

## 2. What it means, and does not

The finding concerns users' primary underlying purpose — not merely the surface
task requested, the assistant's response, or whether the attempt ultimately
succeeds. Two people can issue the identical request for different reasons, so
the task-led form of an interaction does not imply that task completion is
always its ultimate end. The finding also does not assert that every purpose is
equally frequent or that purposes never cluster by domain. It says that
aggregate ordinary use does not support treating any single underlying
objective as predominant.

## 3. How the literature characterizes it

The result is drawn from the genre of work that characterizes real usage in
aggregate: large-scale analyses of anonymized interaction patterns,
economic-index style decompositions of tasks and purposes, and transparency
reporting. Concrete tasks account for much of what users bring to assistants.
Purpose-level estimates need not yield the same distribution across settings
or coding choices. Findings concentrated under topic labels become mixed when
analysts ask why the same task was requested. The literature therefore treats
task-led interaction and heterogeneous purpose as compatible observations at
different levels.

## 4. Second-order implications

Because no single purpose predominates, the assistant's typical interlocutor
cannot be characterized by one default motive beyond having some task at hand.
The same outward request may serve different broader ends, including ends not
recoverable from the text alone. Commentary frames the assistant as general
infrastructure used across a mixture of ends. Its role is adapting to the
immediate task while remaining uncertain about any unstated broader purpose,
not amplifying one characteristic orientation of users as a group.

## 5. Stability and trajectory

The finding is treated as durable at the level of heterogeneity rather than at
the level of a fixed mixture. The composition of purposes can vary over time
and across settings; individual purpose shares can rise or fall with those
shifts. Across such changes, later analyses continue to find task-led
interactions coupled to varied underlying reasons, with no stable cross-setting
objective displacing the rest. The settled premise is not that the mixture is
constant, but that ordinary assistant use remains plural enough that one
predominant underlying purpose is not an adequate aggregate characterization.
